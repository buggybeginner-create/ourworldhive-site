              7. Income tax: old regime against new regime

              For a salaried person, for the current financial year. Fill in your yearly pay and the rest is found from your other tabs.

              Slabs used: new regime has zero tax up to ₹4 lakh and then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% at each next ₹4 lakh, with a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a rebate so salary up to about ₹12.75 lakh pays nothing. Old regime has a ₹50,000 standard deduction, 80C up to ₹1.5 lakh and its usual slabs. Cess of 4% is added. Surcharge for pay above ₹50 lakh and special cases are not included, so check with a tax professional or the income tax site before you file.

              How this is worked out

              Income is your take-home salary, rising by the yearly hike you chose, until the retirement age. Living costs (essentials, lifestyle and protection premiums) rise with inflation. After retirement, living cost is the share of today's cost you chose, still rising with inflation. EMIs and savings-policy premiums stop when they end. Your SIP and any extra SIP step up every year with the hike. PF grows at its own rate and is added to your savings when you retire. Cash left over each year earns 4% and covers any shortfall before funds are touched. Policy payouts are added in the year they arrive. Goals and child-education fees are paid from the funds. Savings means funds plus PF plus cash. Net worth adds policy values and property, and takes off loans. These are estimates, not promises.

              How much money will you have, year by year

              Your salary grows every year. Living cost, home loan EMIs, premiums, child education, goals and SIPs are taken out. Policy payouts and the PLI and bonus are added. What is left is your lump sum.
