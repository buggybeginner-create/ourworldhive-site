Learn the basics

Plain-language ideas for new investors. This is general education, not advice for your situation.

What are a mutual fund, NAV and SIP?

A mutual fund pools money from many people and invests it in shares, bonds or other assets. NAV (net asset value) is the price of one unit of the fund, updated every day. A SIP (systematic investment plan) means putting a fixed amount in on a fixed date each month, so you buy more units when the price is low and fewer when it is high.

Index fund, active fund, direct and regular plans

An index fund copies a market index such as the Nifty 50. An active fund tries to beat an index and usually charges more. A direct plan has no distributor commission, so its yearly cost (expense ratio) is lower than the regular plan of the same fund. Lower cost matters a lot over many years.

Risk, time and falls

Share-based funds can fall a lot, sometimes by a third or more in a bad year, and then take years to recover. Use the "worst fall" number in the Time machine to see this for any fund. Money you will need within a few years does not usually belong in assets that can fall that much.

Spreading risk

Putting everything in one fund or one company is risky. Many people spread money across different kinds of assets such as shares, bonds, gold and cash. How much to put in each depends on your goals, age and comfort with falls, and is a question for a SEBI-registered adviser.

Before you invest
  • Keep an emergency fund, often suggested at 3 to 6 months of expenses.
  • Clear costly debts such as credit-card dues first.
  • Check insurance cover for health and life.
  • Know your goal and how many years you have.
Costs and taxes

Costs include the fund's yearly expense ratio, any exit load, and tax on gains. Tax rules change often, so check the current rules on the Income Tax Department website or ask a tax professional.

Common mistakes
  • Chasing last year's best performer.
  • Stopping a SIP when markets fall.
  • Following tips on social media or messaging groups.
  • Ignoring costs.
  • Investing money you need soon.
How to check if an adviser is registered

Anyone who gives investment advice for a fee must be registered with SEBI. Check the name and registration number on the SEBI website (intermediaries list) and learn more on investor.sebi.gov.in. AMFI publishes mutual fund information at amfiindia.com. Be careful of anyone who promises fixed or guaranteed returns.

Why this page shows delayed data and gives no tips

SEBI rules say that people who are not registered cannot give investment advice or recommendations, and that investor education should not use live or recent market prices to talk about specific securities. So Invest Lab shows history at least 90 days old, never says what to buy or sell, and uses pretend money. If you need advice, speak to a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
